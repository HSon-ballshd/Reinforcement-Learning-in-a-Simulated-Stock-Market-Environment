# 2.9 Expected SARSA

## Overview

**Expected SARSA** is a TD control algorithm that sits between **SARSA** (on-policy, sample one next action) and **Q-learning** (off-policy, take the max). Instead of sampling a single next action like SARSA, or taking the max like Q-learning, it computes the **expected value over the next action under the current policy**.

Think of it this way: SARSA "rolls the dice" once to pick the next action, Q-learning "cheats" by picking the best possible, and Expected SARSA "averages the dice" — it averages over all actions weighted by how likely each one is.

## The Update Rule

The Expected SARSA update for `Q(S, A)` is:

```
Q(S, A) ← Q(S, A) + α [ R + γ · Σ_{a'} π(a'|S') · Q(S', a') − Q(S, A) ]
```

Where:
- **α** — learning rate (step size)
- **R** — immediate reward after taking action `A` in state `S`
- **γ** — discount factor (importance of future rewards)
- **π(a'|S')** — probability of choosing action `a'` in next state `S'` under the current policy
- **Q(S', a')** — estimated value of the next state-action pair

The crucial bit is the **expectation**: `Σ_{a'} π(a'|S') · Q(S', a')` is a weighted average of next-action values, weighted by the policy.

## Comparison with SARSA

### Action Selection
- **SARSA**: chooses next action `A'` via the policy (often ε-greedy) and **samples** it — only one `a'` enters the update.
- **Expected SARSA**: doesn't pick one — it averages over **all** actions according to π.

### Update Rule
- **SARSA**:
  ```
  Q(S,A) ← Q(S,A) + α [ R + γ · Q(S', A') − Q(S,A) ]
  ```
  Uses the single sampled `A'`.
- **Expected SARSA**: replaces `Q(S', A')` with the expectation `Σ_a π(a|S') Q(S', a)`.

### Stability
SARSA's updates depend on which action happened to be sampled, so they can be **noisy and volatile**, especially with stochastic policies or high variance rewards. Expected SARSA **smooths over this noise** by averaging, giving more stable updates.

### Exploration–Exploitation
SARSA uses ε-greedy directly, which can occasionally pick clearly bad actions. Expected SARSA still uses the same policy, but the *update* no longer swings wildly when exploration picks a poor move — the bad action's influence is weighted by its probability.

## Comparison with Q-Learning

### Action Selection at the Next Step
- **Q-learning**: uses `max_{a'} Q(S', a')` — greedy, deterministic.
- **Expected SARSA**: uses `Σ_{a'} π(a'|S') · Q(S', a')` — soft, weighted average.

This means **Expected SARSA generalizes Q-learning**: if π is the greedy policy (probability 1 on the argmax), the expectation collapses to the max, and Expected SARSA becomes Q-learning.

### Update Rules Side-by-Side
| Algorithm | Target |
|---|---|
| SARSA | `R + γ · Q(S', A')` |
| Q-learning | `R + γ · max_{a'} Q(S', a')` |
| Expected SARSA | `R + γ · Σ_{a'} π(a'|S') · Q(S', a')` |

### Stability
Q-learning's `max` operator is a **non-linear** operation that can amplify overestimation bias and produce high-variance targets. Expected SARSA replaces the max with a weighted average — this is a **linear** operation, so the target is less jumpy. The result is typically **more consistent convergence**.

### Exploration–Exploitation
Q-learning decouples the update (max) from the behavior (ε-greedy), which is why it's off-policy. Expected SARSA couples them again by averaging with the same policy it acts with — more on-policy flavor, smoother exploration.

## Why Variance Reduction Matters

Imagine you're learning to drive in a city. SARSA samples the *actual* next move: maybe you get cut off and brake hard, and that one event dominates the update. Q-learning imagines the *best possible* move, which can be unrealistically optimistic. Expected SARSA asks: "**on average**, given how I usually drive, how good is the next situation?" That average is much less sensitive to any single weird event — it's the statistical equivalent of averaging many dice rolls.

Concretely, the update target's variance drops because:
1. The expectation **integrates out** the randomness in next-action selection.
2. You no longer need to *sample* `A'` during the update — the policy supplies the full distribution.

## Key Takeaways

- **Expected SARSA replaces the sampled next-action value in SARSA with an expectation over the next-action distribution under π.**
- The update target is `R + γ · Σ_{a'} π(a'|S') · Q(S', a')`, a **weighted average** rather than a sample or a max.
- It **generalizes Q-learning**: setting π greedy recovers Q-learning exactly; setting π to a softer policy gives a smoother, lower-variance target.
- Compared to SARSA, it is **more stable** because it averages out the noise from sampling one next action.
- Compared to Q-learning, it is **less biased toward overestimation** and tends to converge more smoothly because averaging is a linear operator while max is not.
- The variance reduction comes from replacing a **stochastic sample** (SARSA) or a **non-linear max** (Q-learning) with a **linear expectation** — the dice rolls are averaged, not played.
- Practical takeaway: when your environment is stochastic or you want lower-variance TD targets without leaving on-policy methods, Expected SARSA is often a good middle ground.
